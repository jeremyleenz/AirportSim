# Airport Ops expansion roadmap

Start with a small airport and give players time to learn its operations. Airport improvements relieve bottlenecks. Players choose when to take on more traffic through optional airline contracts.

## 1. Start with a smaller airport (complete for MVP)

The compact starter terminal has two gates, one baggage carousel, two check-in counters, and two security lanes. One counter and one lane are staffed at opening. Players can open the second of each as queues build.

Day 1 starts with one overnight aircraft and a free gate for the first inbound. Early flights are staggered so players can handle an arrival and a departure before more turnarounds overlap. Each of four simulated shifts dispatched all 12 flights before curfew using the starter facilities.

In-game prompts guide players through gate assignment and dispatch as those actions become relevant. The starter carousel is assigned automatically in arrival order.

## 2. Carry the treasury and save progress (complete for MVP)

The closing treasury carries into the next day after base operating costs, extra staffing, ground handling, and fines. The base rate covers one staffed check-in counter, one staffed security lane, and keeping the terminal open. The shift report shows the opening and closing balances, the cost breakdown, and the time spent waiting for a free baggage carousel. At airports with two carousels, it also shows time spent waiting for the player to assign an available carousel.

Progress saves in the browser during each shift, including when players pause or leave the tab. Reopening resumes at the saved time and preserves the pause state. At the end of each shift, the game saves the next day, treasury, timetable, and overnight aircraft. Players can use **Reset progress** in the Terminal tab and confirm to start a fresh Day 1 with the starter airport and treasury.

The base schedule stays available after a loss so players can earn money back through operations.

## 3. Offer the first capacity upgrades (complete for MVP)

Players can buy a second baggage carousel, a third security lane, or a third check-in desk between shifts. A decent first shift can fund one upgrade. Players choose what to buy based on the bottlenecks they encountered. The purchase confirmation previews the facility’s fixed placement.

- **Second carousel** adds capacity near the arrivals exit so two flights can use baggage claim at once. Players then choose which carousel each arrival uses.
- **Third security lane** adds a screening lane beside the café that players can staff when queues build.
- **Third check-in desk** adds a desk with its own queue and baggage belt. The second desk shifts toward baggage claim to make room.

Each completed purchase saves the updated treasury and the facility’s placement. Upgrades are available the next morning and are restored when the game reopens. The base schedule remains available after each upgrade.

## 4. Add airport layout customisation

Give players a planning mode between shifts to place new facilities and relocate existing ones. Gates, check-in counters, security lanes, baggage carousels, seating, shops, and other amenities all use the same planning mode. Apply the same principle to larger facilities, including terminals and runways, as they become available.

Let players draft several changes together and check that the resulting layout works before they apply it. Gates need a passenger route and access to the taxiway. Check-in and security need queue space and a route from the public terminal through screening to the gates. Carousels need passenger access and a baggage handling connection. Explain which connection or space a placement needs when it cannot be used.

Show the cost and affected passenger routes before players apply a change. Let them undo moves so they can try different placements and recover from an accidental tap on mobile. Show the consequences during the next shift, such as shorter walks to gates or less congestion around check-in.

Extend the browser save to cover every facility the player can place or move. Save the facility positions and updated treasury when the player applies the layout, and restore that layout on reopening.

## 5. Add a third gate and expand capacity further

Offer a third gate. Show when inbound flights held because all gates were occupied so players can judge when they need another stand. Use queue and baggage wait data to guide any further expansion of check-in, security, or baggage claim.

Use the planning mode to fit added capacity into the airport. A new gate, for example, may require extending the concourse and connecting the stand to a taxiway.

## 6. Introduce optional airline contracts

Start with one base airline and offer a small optional contract between shifts. Show the proposed arrival and departure times, passenger and baggage volumes, and expected revenue. A contract in a quieter window may fit the current airport. A contract during a busy arrival bank may justify building capacity first.

The base timetable can vary day to day. Players can operate the base schedule or choose when to add flights by signing optional contracts. Buying an upgrade leaves that choice with the player.

Let players drop optional contracts between shifts to reduce traffic when they need time to recover.

## 7. Add richer amenities

Passengers already use the café, shared seating, and toilets. Future upgrades give players more control over amenity capacity and placement and make the effects visible during a shift.

Start with a small **retail kiosk**. Use existing passenger wait time to test spending and concession revenue before developing a full restaurant.

- **Restaurants** once the spending model supports a fuller food and drink service.
- **Additional shared seating** once passenger satisfaction has a clear effect on gameplay. Let players place seats in accessible waiting areas and see where passengers use them.
- **Additional toilets** once restroom availability has a clear effect on the passenger experience.
- **Travelators** once walking distances can affect whether passengers reach their gates on time.

## Longer-term ideas

- **Additional runways** move up when flights regularly wait for runway slots despite adequate gate and staffing capacity.
- **Immigration** adds passport control alongside the first international contract that requires it.
- **Parking** becomes a capacity and revenue decision once how passengers reach the airport affects gameplay.
- **Additional terminals** become worthwhile when assigning flights and facilities across buildings gives players a meaningful choice.
- **Ground transportation** adds bus, rail, or shuttle connections once travel to the airport influences when passengers reach the terminal.
